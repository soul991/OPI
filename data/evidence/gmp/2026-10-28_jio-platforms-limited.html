IPO Grey Market Premium (GMP)

The grey market premium (GMP) is the price at which an IPO is traded in an unofficial/unregulated grey market prior to its listing. The GMP reflects how a particular company's IPO issue might react on the day of listing. A positive GMP signals that the IPO is likely to list at a profit, while a negative GMP indicates a likely discount on listing.

IPO GMP is subject to extreme volatility — an investment decision based solely on Jio Platforms IPO GMP will prove risky. Consider all factors before deciding whether to invest in Jio Platforms IPO.

Fixed inputs: buy prices ₹1,065 employee (−₹54) / ₹1,119 others; ₹8,72,820 blocked; ₹8,48,844 cash paid if fully allotted; ₹1,27,180 idle.

Allotment model used (stated so you can challenge it)

- Category ≤ 1.0× → full allotment of what you applied.
- Category > 1.0× → Retail & Shareholder cap at 1 lot (12 sh) via lottery, expected ≈ 12 ÷ S.
- Employee: ≤1× → full 444; >1× → only the ₹2L tier competes (~12 ÷ S), and the 276 sh above ₹2L get nothing.

What the numbers say

1. Subscription dwarfs listing price. Moving across an entire row (loss → bull case) changes P/L less than moving down one column (A→C). Your result is decided on 23 Oct at close, not on listing day.
2. The employee pool is ~95% of the upside — it's the only one that can hold 444 shares and the only one carrying the ₹54/share edge, but only while employee subscription stays ≤1× (0.90× on day 1). The moment it crosses 1×, you drop from 444 to ~1 lot and the whole top row collapses toward row C.
3. Retail is the weakest rupee: 168 shares applied, but expected ~1 lot or less in any oversubscribed close — same ₹1.88L blocked as shareholder for a structurally similar payoff.
4. Downside is bounded by the ₹54 discount on the employee shares: in every scenario a flat ₹1,119 listing still nets positive (the discount), and a true loss needs the price to fall below ₹1,065.
